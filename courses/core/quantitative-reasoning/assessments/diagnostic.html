CORE-103 Readiness Diagnostic

Readiness evidence. Show calculations, units, assumptions and interpretation. Written work requires review.

1. Demonstrate units, magnitude and estimation: Use units, orders of magnitude and estimation to test numerical plausibility. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

2. Demonstrate ratios, rates and proportions: Interpret part-to-part, part-to-whole and per-unit relationships. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

3. Demonstrate percent change and percentage points: Distinguish percent change from absolute and percentage-point change. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

4. Demonstrate reading tables and structured data: Extract comparisons, totals, rates and missing context from tables. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

5. Demonstrate axes, scales and graph integrity: Detect truncated axes, uneven scales and visual exaggeration. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

6. Demonstrate choosing effective displays: Match bar charts, line charts, histograms and scatterplots to the question. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

7. Demonstrate mean, median and mode: Choose and interpret measures of center. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

8. Demonstrate range, iqr and standard deviation: Compare measures of variability and sensitivity to outliers. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

9. Demonstrate shape, outliers and distribution stories: Describe skew, clusters, gaps and unusual observations. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

10. Demonstrate population, sample and sampling frame: Distinguish target populations from observed samples. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

11. Demonstrate bias, nonresponse and convenience samples: Identify common pathways from sampling design to misleading conclusions. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

12. Demonstrate measurement, variables and operational definitions: Define variables so that measurements match the intended concept. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

13. Demonstrate events, complements and simple probability: Model basic chance events and complements. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

14. Demonstrate conditional probability and independence: Interpret probabilities that change with new information. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

15. Demonstrate expected value and long-run reasoning: Use weighted outcomes to compare uncertain choices. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

16. Demonstrate scatterplots and association: Describe direction, form and strength in paired quantitative data. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

17. Demonstrate correlation and its limits: Interpret correlation without treating it as causation or complete description. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

18. Demonstrate confounding and alternative explanations: Identify variables that may create or distort observed associations. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

19. Demonstrate absolute risk and relative risk: Translate relative claims into underlying frequencies. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.

20. Demonstrate base rates and denominators: Ask what population and denominator a rate actually describes. Use or construct a realistic numerical example and explain the result.
