KHL-CORE-102 · Module 08 · Lesson 03

Conflicts of Interest and Incentives

Evaluate incentives without assuming that incentives automatically invalidate evidence.

Reasoning target

Use this skill to make reasoning more explicit, testable and revisable.

Concept frame

How to reason with information we cannot personally verify from first principles.

Critical-thinking rule: do not confuse confidence with evidence. State the claim, identify the support, expose assumptions, compare alternatives, and preserve uncertainty when uncertainty is warranted.

Practice

State the reasoning skill in your own words and give one valid example.
Create a flawed example involving evaluate incentives without assuming that incentives automatically invalidate evidence. and explain exactly where it fails.
Apply the lesson to one claim drawn from science, civics, health, business or the arts.
Identify what additional evidence would make the conclusion stronger or weaker.
Write one sentence describing what evidence would cause you to revise your current judgment.

Evidence audit

Choose one real-world claim. Record the source, exact wording of the claim, strongest supporting evidence, strongest limitation, one plausible alternative explanation, and your current confidence level.

Reflection

Which part of your reasoning was most vulnerable to error: definitions, evidence selection, inference, causation, uncertainty, or values?
