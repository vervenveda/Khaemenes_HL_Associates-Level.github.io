CORE-102 Midterm

Cumulative Modules 1–6. Written reasoning requires evaluator review. Required knowledge gates use 80% mastery.

1. Analyze a claim involving Claims, Questions and Testable Statements. Distinguish assertions, questions, definitions and statements that can be evaluated. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

2. Analyze a claim involving Definitions, Boundaries and Ambiguity. Test whether a definition is clear enough to separate examples from nonexamples. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

3. Analyze a claim involving Necessary, Sufficient and Relevant Conditions. Separate what must be true, what is enough, and what merely happens to be associated. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

4. Analyze a claim involving Premises, Conclusions and Argument Form. Map an argument into reasons and conclusion. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

5. Analyze a claim involving Validity, Soundness and Strength. Distinguish deductive validity from truth and inductive strength. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

6. Analyze a claim involving Converse, Contrapositive and Conditional Error. Recognize valid and invalid transformations of conditional reasoning. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

7. Analyze a claim involving Evidence Types and Fit. Match observations, documents, experiments, statistics, testimony and models to appropriate claims. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

8. Analyze a claim involving Burden of Proof and Extraordinary Claims. Identify who is responsible for supporting a claim and how evidentiary demands change with stakes. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

9. Analyze a claim involving Absence of Evidence and Evidence of Absence. Distinguish missing evidence from positive evidence against a claim. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

10. Analyze a claim involving Universal Claims and Counterexamples. Use counterexamples to test broad claims. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

11. Analyze a claim involving Boundary Cases and Stress Tests. Probe definitions and arguments at extreme or unusual cases. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

12. Analyze a claim involving Refining a Claim After Failure. Revise an overbroad statement without abandoning defensible evidence. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

13. Analyze a claim involving Relevance Fallacies. Diagnose ad hominem, red herring, appeal to emotion and distraction. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

14. Analyze a claim involving Weak Induction and Overgeneralization. Identify hasty generalization, cherry-picking and anecdotal substitution. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

15. Analyze a claim involving False Choice, Circularity and Loaded Framing. Recognize false dilemmas, begging the question and premises hidden in wording. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

16. Analyze a claim involving Correlation Versus Causation. Avoid causal conclusions from association alone. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

17. Analyze a claim involving Mechanisms, Confounders and Alternative Explanations. Generate plausible competing causes and identify missing variables. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

18. Analyze a claim involving Causal Evidence and Natural Experiments. Compare observational and experimental evidence for causal inference. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

19. Analyze a claim involving Base Rates and Conditional Probability. Use background rates to interpret evidence. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.

20. Analyze a claim involving Relative Risk, Absolute Risk and Framing. Translate risk claims into comparable forms. State the conclusion, support, assumptions, alternative explanation or objection, and what evidence would change the judgment.
