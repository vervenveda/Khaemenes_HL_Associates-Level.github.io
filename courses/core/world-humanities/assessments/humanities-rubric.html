Comparative Humanities Rubric

  1. Primary work/source is accurately described.
  2. Interpretation uses specific evidence.
  3. Historical/cultural context is relevant and documented.
  4. Comparison avoids flattening important differences.
  5. Translation/edition/mediation issues are identified where relevant.
  6. Ethical or philosophical reasoning is explicit.
  7. Missing voices and archival limits are acknowledged.
  8. Sources are attributed and traceable.
  9. Argument is coherent without claiming a single universal interpretation.
  10. Reflection explains how comparison/context changed understanding.
