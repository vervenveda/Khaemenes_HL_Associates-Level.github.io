CORE-108 Readiness Diagnostic

Readiness evidence. Interpretive claims require specific source evidence and contextual reasoning.

1. Analyze or compare What Counts as a Humanities Question?: Distinguish interpretive, historical, ethical and aesthetic questions from purely factual ones. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

2. Analyze or compare Texts, Artifacts and Contexts: Treat written, visual, oral and material works as evidence situated in time and culture. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

3. Analyze or compare Interpretation and Evidence: Support interpretation with specific features of a work and relevant context. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

4. Analyze or compare Myth, Epic and Foundational Narrative: Compare how origin stories encode values, conflict and social imagination. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

5. Analyze or compare Oral Tradition and Cultural Memory: Study how memory changes when knowledge moves through performance and retelling. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

6. Analyze or compare Archive, Silence and Survival: Ask whose voices are preserved, omitted or mediated by surviving evidence. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

7. Analyze or compare Voice, Perspective and Reliability: Analyze who speaks, from where, and with what limits. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

8. Analyze or compare Image, Symbol and Motif: Interpret recurring language and imagery without reducing a work to one fixed meaning. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

9. Analyze or compare Form, Genre and Expectation: Examine how genre shapes what readers anticipate and how meaning is produced. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

10. Analyze or compare Knowledge, Doubt and Justification: Compare ways traditions distinguish belief, knowledge and wisdom. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

11. Analyze or compare Self, Mind and Identity: Explore competing accounts of personhood and continuity. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

12. Analyze or compare Meaning, Mortality and the Good Life: Compare ethical and existential approaches to living well. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

13. Analyze or compare Consequences, Duties and Rules: Compare outcome-based and duty-based moral reasoning. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

14. Analyze or compare Virtue, Character and Human Flourishing: Examine ethical traditions centered on habits, character and flourishing. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

15. Analyze or compare Care, Relationship and Responsibility: Analyze moral reasoning grounded in dependency, care and social relationship. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

16. Analyze or compare Authority and Legitimacy: Compare accounts of why political or social authority should be obeyed or resisted. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

17. Analyze or compare Freedom, Equality and Justice: Examine tensions among liberty, equality, rights and social obligation. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

18. Analyze or compare Empire, Resistance and Liberation: Study how texts and thinkers interpret domination, resistance and decolonization. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

19. Analyze or compare Ritual, Practice and Community: Study religion through lived practice as well as doctrine. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.

20. Analyze or compare Sacred Texts and Interpretation: Compare interpretive traditions without treating one reading as the only possible one. Use a specific work/source and identify relevant context plus one limitation.
