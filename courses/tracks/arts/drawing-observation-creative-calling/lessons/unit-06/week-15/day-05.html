About This Marketing & Promotion Day

Present or test the Week 15 professional asset through Architectural Viewpoint Review. Use a defined audience and a useful question. Public posting is optional; private, classroom, family, educator, or offline review fully satisfies the lesson.

Adapted from the preserved Bazaar Art ART101 · Week 15 source lesson.

Marketing & Promotion Day Goals

Define the Audience

Choose a reviewer appropriate to the work and its privacy/context needs.

Prepare the Message

Explain the purpose without overclaiming.

Ask a Useful Question

Test comprehension, usability, hierarchy, evidence, or context—not taste.

Learn from Response

Record useful signals and ignore vanity metrics as mastery criteria.

Communication Principle · Choose Channel
01 · Lead with PurposeState what the work or asset is meant to communicate or accomplish.
02 · Choose the ChannelUse in-person, print, email, private message, controlled portfolio, classroom, or public channel only when appropriate.
03 · Protect ContextDo not strip away source, consent, uncertainty, or limitation information.
04 · Ask One Specific QuestionTest the week’s intended viewer understanding.
05 · Avoid Popularity ScoringLikes, follows, reach, and virality are not mastery evidence.
Prepare · Preview · Present
01 · PrepareOpen the finished Thursday asset and supporting evidence.
02 · PreviewCheck readability, privacy, source notes, captions, and file quality.
03 · PresentLet the viewer encounter the work without coaching the answer.
04 · AskUse a question tied to Architectural Viewpoint Review.
05 · RecordCapture the response accurately.
06 · Follow UpChoose one evidence-based improvement to preserve or test next.
Response Tracker · Signal vs Vanity Metrics

Useful Signal

Specific evidence about comprehension, hierarchy, usability, context, or a visible problem.

Weak Signal

General praise/dislike may be emotionally relevant but often lacks actionable detail.

Vanity Metric

Likes, followers, impressions, and virality do not determine mastery.

Follow-Up

Decide what to keep, revise, or test next without automatically obeying every opinion.
