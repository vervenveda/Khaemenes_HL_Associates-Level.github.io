About This Lesson

The color we perceive is shaped by light, material, surrounding colors, and context. This lesson connects direct observation with responsible research so color meaning is supported by evidence rather than stereotypes or universal claims.

Adapted from Bazaar Art ART101 · Week 07 · Color, Place and Meaning.

Lesson Goals

Observe Light

Compare the same place or subject under different lighting or surrounding conditions.

Notice Material

Record how surfaces reflect, absorb, or scatter light differently.

Research Context

Investigate one specific contextual meaning of color without stereotyping.

Create from Place

Use color and value intentionally to communicate atmosphere or relationship to place.

Materials

Meaningful Place

Choose a safely accessible place, view, or well-documented remembered location.

Color Medium

Use physical or offline digital media for two studies and one finished work.

Research Source

Use a museum, archive, institutional record, or credible publication.

Evidence Device

Document both studies, the final artwork, and the source citation.

Creative Action Steps
01 · Choose the PlaceRecord what is directly observed and distinguish it from memory or interpretation.
02 · Study Condition OneMake a color observation study under one lighting or surrounding condition.
03 · Study Condition TwoRepeat the subject under a different condition, or use a carefully labeled reference and note the limitation.
04 · Research One ContextInvestigate one artwork, object, or practice in which color has a documented contextual meaning.
05 · Create the Finished WorkUse value structure and color relationships from Weeks 5–6 to communicate a specific atmosphere or relationship to place.
06 · Write the Artist StatementExplain observations, source context, choices, uncertainty, and what should not be generalized.
Required Lesson Evidence

Complete every field and attach both studies plus the final artwork.

Knowledge Check · 80% Mastery

Answer all five questions. Four correct answers are required.

1. What can change a surface color's appearance without changing its pigment?
2. What role do surrounding colors play?
3. Which is the strongest claim about color symbolism?
4. What is a limitation of a screen-based color simulation?
5. What is a careful distinction in cultural research?
Complete the lesson evidence and knowledge check to evaluate mastery.
