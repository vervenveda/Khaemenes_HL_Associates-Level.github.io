About This Lesson

Value is the relative lightness or darkness of a tone. When artists observe a consistent light source, organized value relationships can make a flat drawing describe volume. Today you will build a five-step value scale, identify light and shadow families, create a form study, and revise one tonal relationship from evidence.

Adapted from Bazaar Art ART101 · Week 04 · Value and Form.

Lesson Goals

Identify Value

Distinguish clearly separated light-to-dark relationships.

Read the Light

Observe light direction before shading.

Build Volume

Use highlight, form shadow, core shadow, reflected light, and cast shadow purposefully.

Revise

Preserve an early study and make one specific tonal correction.

Materials

Simple Form

Use a sphere-like object, cup, fruit, box, or another clearly lit form.

Single Light Source

A window or safe lamp can provide a consistent direction of light.

Drawing Materials

Graphite, charcoal, or another medium capable of several distinct values.

Evidence Device

Photograph, scan, or export the value scale and form study.

Creative Action Steps
01 · Observe Light DirectionBefore shading, identify where the strongest light comes from and where the object blocks it.
02 · Build Five ValuesCreate a five-step scale from light to dark with clearly distinguishable steps.
03 · Map the FormLightly identify the light-facing area, form shadow/core shadow, reflected light, and cast shadow.
04 · Shade the StudyUse the scale to develop a simple observed form without outlining every tonal boundary.
05 · CompareCheck whether adjacent values are distinct and whether the cast shadow agrees with the light direction.
06 · Revise One RelationshipMake one targeted change—such as darkening the core shadow—and record the reason.
