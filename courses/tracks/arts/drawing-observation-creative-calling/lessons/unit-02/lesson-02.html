About This Lesson

Proportion compares the sizes of parts within a whole. Instead of guessing, artists can use one observed dimension as a reference, compare height-to-width relationships, and inspect negative spaces to reveal placement errors. Today you will build a small still life, measure relationships, preserve an early attempt, and revise from visible evidence.

Adapted from Bazaar Art ART101 · Week 03 · Proportion and Relationships.

Lesson Goals

Compare

Use one observed dimension as a reference unit for other heights and widths.

Measure

Record relative relationships rather than copying example numbers.

Use Negative Space

Compare the shapes around and between objects as evidence.

Revise

Preserve an initial study and document one specific proportional correction.

Materials

Three Objects

Arrange a mug, book, and another safe household object or a similar three-object still life.

Drawing Materials

Pencil, eraser, paper or sketchbook; a digital equivalent is acceptable.

Measuring Aid

Use a pencil held consistently at arm’s length or a ruler/straightedge for recorded comparisons.

Evidence Device

Photograph, scan, or export both the early and revised drawing.

Creative Action Steps
01 · ArrangeSet up a three-object still life and choose one dimension as your reference unit.
02 · ThumbnailMake a quick first sketch before measuring. Preserve it.
03 · CompareRecord at least four relative measurements such as height-to-width or object-to-object relationships.
04 · Check Negative SpaceIdentify and compare at least two spaces around or between the objects.
05 · RedrawCreate a revised observational drawing using the measurements and negative-space checks.
06 · Explain the CorrectionIdentify the largest proportional change and explain what visible comparison justified it.

Knowledge Check · 80% Mastery

Answer all five questions. Four correct answers are required.

1. What does proportion describe in observational drawing?
2. If a mug is three-quarters as tall as a book, what is its height ratio to the book?
3. What is negative space?
4. What does a reference unit accomplish?
5. Why should an initial sketch be kept?
