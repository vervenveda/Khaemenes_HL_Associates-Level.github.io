About This Lesson

Contour drawing trains the eye to notice visible boundaries, overlaps, changes in direction, and internal edges instead of relying on a memorized symbol for an object. Today you will slow down, look carefully, compare three contour approaches, and preserve the differences as evidence of growth.

Adapted from Bazaar Art ART101 · Week 02 · Contour and Careful Seeing.

Lesson Goals

See the Edge

Distinguish an observed boundary from the simplified symbol your memory expects.

Slow the Eye

Coordinate eye movement and mark-making through blind and modified contour practice.

Compare

Preserve multiple studies so you can identify specific changes in accuracy, direction, and attention.

Revise Intentionally

Correct selected edge relationships without erasing the evidence of your earlier process.

Materials

Observed Object

Choose an ordinary object with a distinctive silhouette: a shoe, cup, leaf, key, tool, or similar subject.

Drawing Surface

Use several sheets of paper, a sketchbook, or an accessible digital drawing surface.

Drawing Tool

Pencil, pen, charcoal, or stylus. Avoid an eraser during the first two contour studies.

Evidence Device

Use a camera, scanner, or export function to document all three studies clearly.

Creative Action Steps
01 · Observe Before DrawingTrace the object’s silhouette with your eyes. Notice overlaps, internal edges, openings, curves, angles, and direction changes.
02 · Blind Contour · About 2 MinutesKeep your attention on the object instead of the page. Let the line wander. Do not erase or restart.
03 · Modified Contour · About 5 MinutesLook back and forth deliberately. Compare angle, proportion, openings, and edge direction while allowing occasional page checks.
04 · Final Observational ContourCreate a third drawing with selective line weight. Use stronger or quieter lines intentionally rather than outlining everything equally.
05 · Compare the Three StudiesIdentify at least three places where your observation changed. Preserve all three drawings instead of replacing the earlier attempts.
06 · Revise One RelationshipChoose one edge, angle, overlap, or proportion to correct. Record what changed and why.
Required Lesson Evidence

Complete every field and attach evidence of the three contour studies. Missing work cannot be averaged away.

Knowledge Check · 80% Mastery

Answer all five questions. Four correct answers are required.

1. What is the main purpose of contour drawing?
2. During a blind contour study, where should most attention remain?
3. What distinguishes modified contour from blind contour?
4. Why preserve the first contour study?
5. Which statement is an observation rather than an interpretation?
Complete the lesson evidence and knowledge check to evaluate mastery.
