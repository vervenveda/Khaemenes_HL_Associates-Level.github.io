About This Lesson

Balance is the distribution of visual weight, not a requirement for identical placement. Unequal forms can still feel balanced through scale, contrast, color, position, negative space, and repetition. Movement emerges when those relationships guide the eye through the composition.

Adapted from Bazaar Art ART101 · Week 09 · Balance and Movement.

Lesson Goals

Read Visual Weight

Compare how size, contrast, color, and placement affect emphasis.

Balance Unequal Forms

Use asymmetry intentionally instead of relying on exact mirroring.

Create Movement

Use diagonals, repetition, and variation as directional cues.

Revise

Preserve before-and-after evidence and explain one change.

Materials

Simple Motifs

Use a few shapes, objects, or repeated forms that can be rearranged.

Thumbnail Surface

Prepare space for three exploratory compositions.

Art Medium

Use accessible drawing, painting, collage, or offline digital tools.

Evidence Device

Document thumbnails, final composition, and revision.

Creative Action Steps
01 · Explore SymmetryMake one composition organized around an axis or approximate mirror balance.
02 · Explore AsymmetryBalance a larger muted form with smaller high-contrast elements.
03 · Explore MovementUse diagonals, repetition, interval, or gaze to direct the eye.
04 · Compare in GrayscaleCheck visual weight without relying only on hue.
05 · Create the Final CompositionDevelop the strongest study into an original artwork with intentional balance and movement.
06 · Revise and ReflectPreserve both versions and explain which relationship changed.
