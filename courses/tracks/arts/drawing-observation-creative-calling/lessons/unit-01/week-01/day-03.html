About This Skills Day

Today you strengthen the technical habits underneath observational drawing: looking before marking, comparing height and width, checking angles, noticing negative space, and correcting from visible evidence instead of memory.

Skills & Technique Day supports Week 01 · Baseline Observation.

Skills & Technique Goals

Slow the Eye

Look deliberately before making a mark.

Compare Relationships

Use relative height, width, angle, and placement rather than guessing isolated parts.

Use Negative Space

Compare the spaces around and between forms as evidence.

Transfer the Skill

Apply one observation correction to Monday’s baseline drawing or a fresh copy-study of the same subject.

Skill of the Day

Visual Target

Produce a small observed study whose proportions and placements come from comparison rather than symbol drawing.

Prerequisite Check

Reopen Monday’s baseline evidence and identify one area where you guessed rather than compared.

Concept

Observation improves when the artist compares relationships: this width to that height, this angle to that edge, this object to the space beside it.

Common Mistake

Drawing what an object “should” look like instead of checking what is actually visible.

Segmented Demonstration & Key Cues
01 · Look FirstPause before each major mark and identify the visible relationship you are about to record.
02 · Compare Height & WidthChoose one dimension as a reference and compare others against it.
03 · Check AnglesCompare the direction of slanted edges instead of naming them from memory.
04 · Check Negative SpaceLook at the shapes beside and between forms to test placement.
05 · Correct SelectivelyChange only the relationship that the evidence shows is inaccurate.
Practice Sequence
Warm-UpMake three one-minute shape studies of the same object without detail.
Controlled ExerciseRecord one height-to-width comparison, two angles, and two negative spaces.
Guided PracticeCreate a 10-minute observed study using those comparisons.
Self-ComparisonCompare the new study with Monday’s baseline and identify one measurable improvement.
RetryIf a relationship still feels guessed, compare again and redraw only that area.
TransferApply one observation correction to the active Week 1 evidence set.

2. What is negative space?
3. Why compare angles?
4. What is a useful self-comparison?
5. What should happen when one relationship is wrong?
