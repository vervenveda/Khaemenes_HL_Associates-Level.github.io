About This Creative Day

Artists often carry efficient visual shorthand for familiar things: an eye becomes an almond shape, a mug becomes a cylinder, a tree becomes a lollipop. Week 2 trains the opposite habit. You will compare what memory predicts with what observation actually shows, then build a revised drawing from edges, angles, intervals, negative spaces and proportion evidence.

Year 2 follows the Bazaar Art studio sequence: observe → research → experiment → create/revise → reflect/archive. Artistic taste is never scored.

Creative Day Goals

Creative Action Steps & Worked Demonstration

Worked Example · Cup Symbol vs Cup Observed

The memory cup is tidy and symmetrical because the mind knows “cup.” The observed cup is less generic: the ellipse tilts, the sides taper unevenly, the handle opening has a specific shape, and the base angle differs from the rim. Week 2 asks you to draw those relationships instead of correcting the subject into an icon.

01 · Memory SketchWithout looking for more than a few seconds, make a 3-minute shorthand sketch of the familiar object.
02 · Reset & ObservePlace the object at one fixed angle and spend 90 seconds looking before drawing.
03 · Edge MapTrace the outer contour slowly and note where edges change direction, disappear or overlap.
04 · Angle & Interval CheckRecord at least four angle comparisons and three spacing/proportion comparisons.
05 · Negative-Space StudyDraw at least two empty shapes around or inside the subject.
06 · 20-Minute Observed DrawingBuild a second study from the recorded relationships.
07 · Compare & ReviseCircle one shorthand assumption and visibly correct it from evidence.
Required Lesson Evidence
Vocabulary

Visual Shorthand

A simplified remembered symbol used in place of close observation.

Edge

A visible boundary or transition that may be hard, soft, lost or interrupted.

Angle

The directional relationship of one line or edge to another.

Interval

The measurable space between landmarks or forms.

Negative Space

The shape of space around and between positive forms.

Comparative Seeing

Judging one visible relationship against another.

Reflection & Archive

Archive the memory sketch, observed study, notes and revision together.
