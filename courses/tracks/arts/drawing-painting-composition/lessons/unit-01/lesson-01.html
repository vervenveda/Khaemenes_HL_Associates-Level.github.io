About This Lesson

Year 2 begins by measuring control, not talent. You will create one sustained observation drawing and one controlled technical study, then compare them, identify a specific weakness, revise one relationship, and archive the result as the first professional evidence record of ARTS-211.

This follows the Bazaar Art studio sequence: observe → research → experiment → create/revise → reflect/archive. Artistic taste is not scored.

Lesson Goals

Observe Precisely

Work from a real subject and record proportion, edge, value, space and visual relationships rather than remembered symbols.

Test Control

Separate line pressure, value range and material handling into a controlled diagnostic study.

Revise from Evidence

Use comparison rather than preference to choose one relationship for correction.

Establish Year 2 Practice

Begin a professional habit of naming, dating, documenting, critiquing and archiving studio work.

Materials & Studio Setup

Drawing Surface

Sketchbook, drawing paper, tablet, or another surface large enough for a sustained study.

Primary Tool

Graphite, charcoal, ink, stylus, or another direct drawing medium already available.

Observed Subject

A still life of two or three real objects with overlapping forms, varied edges and at least three visible value groups.

Evidence Method

Camera, scanner, screenshot, or clear written/drawn documentation. No purchase is required.

Use safe materials and a stable work area. Do not use solvent-based media or unsafe tools for this baseline.
Creative Action Steps & Worked Demonstration

Worked Example · Before Detail

Block the largest masses first. Check the cup height against the box width, compare the highest and lowest points, then identify only three major value groups. If the box angle is wrong, correct that relationship before adding small details.

Year 2 principle: detail does not repair weak structure. Compare, diagnose, revise.

01 · Observe for 2 MinutesDo not draw. Record the largest shape, dominant angle, strongest overlap, darkest value and largest negative space.
02 · 5-Minute Construction ThumbnailBlock the arrangement with simple masses, axes and landmarks. Keep corrections visible.
03 · 30-Minute Observed DrawingDevelop proportion, overlap, edge and a minimum five-value range. Work from observation, not from a photograph unless accessibility requires it.
04 · Controlled Technique StripCreate three 5×5 cm tests: line-pressure range, five-value scale, and one material/mark-family test.
05 · Compare & DiagnoseName one strength and one structural weakness. The weakness must be specific: proportion, edge, value separation, alignment, spacing or material control.
06 · Revise One RelationshipMake one visible correction in the drawing or a small revision study. Do not redraw the entire piece just to make it cleaner.
07 · ArchiveDocument the thumbnail, sustained drawing, technique strip and revision together.

Vocabulary

Construction

Simple underlying masses, axes and landmarks used to establish structure before detail.

Proportion

The size relationship between one part or object and another.

Edge

A visible boundary or transition; edges may appear hard, soft, lost or interrupted.

Value Range

The span from the lightest to darkest visible tones used in an artwork.

Negative Space

The shape and interval of space around and between forms.

Material Control

Intentional handling of pressure, direction, density, tool and surface.

Critique

Evidence-based analysis used to identify what works, what is unclear and what can be revised.

Revision

An intentional change made after comparison, critique or testing.
