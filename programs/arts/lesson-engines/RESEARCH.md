# Arts Online Learning Engine Research

## Research conclusion

The five-day Arts schedule should use **five different lesson engines** because the learner is performing five different kinds of work.

The strongest online art-learning patterns are not long lectures or giant content libraries. They are:

1. clear weekly structure
2. short, segmented instruction
3. visual demonstrations
4. immediate hands-on practice
5. authentic projects
6. visible progress
7. clear next action
8. structured reflection
9. portfolio/evidence capture
10. feedback from relevant audiences
11. accessibility and low technical friction

## Evidence translated into design rules

### Segment rather than overload

Multimedia-learning research consistently supports learner-paced segmentation, signaling of key information, removal of unnecessary material and integrated text/visuals. Therefore no engine should open with a wall of text or a long uninterrupted lecture.

**Rule:** one page = one dominant job.

### Show before asking

Online art programs such as Proko and SVS repeatedly combine lecture, demonstration, assignment and critique. Visual skill instruction is stronger when students can see the process and then try it themselves.

**Rule:** technique engines use Explain → Demonstrate → Try → Compare → Apply.

### Authentic production over passive completion

Art-specific online-learning guidance emphasizes authentic creative work, multiple assessment forms, critique/reflection and digital documentation.

**Rule:** completion is based on evidence produced, not merely watching or clicking.

### Clear weekly modules and progress

Rutgers Arts Online uses weekly modules that combine lectures, exercises, critiques, assessments and project milestones. Strong platforms also make the next step obvious.

**Rule:** each engine must show Today’s Role, Today’s Goal, Today’s Output and the next action above the fold.

### Cognitive load must stay low

The interface should remove decorative distractions, chunk instructions, keep text adjacent to the image/demo it explains and let learners control video pace.

**Rule:** no crowded dashboards inside the active learning area.

### Art learning needs flexibility

Professional online art programs commonly allow different media while teaching transferable principles.

**Rule:** assign the same artistic problem, not the same artwork.

### Public feedback is legitimate learning

Online art communities explicitly encourage sharing work with family, social platforms or critique groups and considering whether the work achieves the artist's intention.

**Khaemenes adaptation:** feedback is gathered through real-world channels and documented; likes/follower counts are not mastery.

## Universal page anatomy

Every engine shares only a small common frame:

1. canonical VNV/Khaemenes chrome
2. breadcrumb
3. day identity / role
4. one-sentence purpose
5. progress indicator
6. Today's Goal
7. Today's Output
8. active work area
9. evidence capture
10. reflection / next action
11. accessibility / materials / time notes
12. save/export to portfolio or business record where appropriate

The active work area changes by engine.

## Five-engine model

### 1. Creative Day Engine

**Cognitive mode:** divergent thinking / production / flow.

The page should get out of the way.

Recommended order:

- Creative Prompt
- Why It Matters — maximum 2–3 sentences
- Inspiration Strip — 2–4 purposeful references, never decorative clutter
- Constraints / Freedom
- Make Now button
- optional timer / focus block
- process-photo checkpoint
- Midpoint question: Continue / Change / Abandon and restart
- Finish or Stop Intentionally
- photograph/scan/save
- 3-question reflection
- portfolio candidate toggle

Avoid:
- quizzes
- lengthy lecture
- excessive navigation
- mandatory imitation of an example
- grading taste

### 2. CEO Day Engine

**Cognitive mode:** planning / prioritization / management.

Recommended order:

- CEO Dashboard
- This Week at a Glance
- Money snapshot
- inventory snapshot
- deadlines/opportunities
- unfinished admin
- Top 3 CEO Decisions
- one guided management lesson
- action checklist
- update records
- calendar/deadline actions
- Artpreneurship Growth Fund check
- CEO Notes / decision log
- next-week priorities

The page should feel like running a small studio business, not taking a business quiz.

### 3. Skills & Technique Day Engine

**Cognitive mode:** deliberate practice.

Recommended order:

- Skill of the Day
- visual target / finished example
- prerequisite check
- short concept explanation
- segmented demonstration
- key cues shown beside the demo
- warm-up
- controlled exercise
- guided practice
- independent application
- self-comparison checklist
- common mistakes
- repeat / retry option
- save skill study
- transfer prompt: use this skill in your own work

This is the engine that should most closely resemble a traditional teaching lesson.

### 4. Artpreneur Day Engine

**Cognitive mode:** build / apply / launch business infrastructure.

Recommended order:

- Business Asset of the Day
- why the asset matters
- finished example
- short checklist of requirements
- step-by-step build-along
- decision points rather than one prescribed answer
- real-world research field
- cost/time field where relevant
- build the actual asset
- preview/test
- export/download/publish
- record where it lives
- maintenance reminder
- next business action

Examples:
website, portfolio, business card, pricing sheet, opportunity tracker, application packet, event kit.

### 5. Marketing & Promotion Day Engine

**Cognitive mode:** audience / communication / outreach / evidence.

Recommended order:

- Marketing Goal
- Audience
- Work/offer being promoted
- one communication principle
- choose channel
- build promotional asset
- preview accessibility/readability
- publish/share/send or prepare offline equivalent
- record where and when
- structured feedback question
- response tracker
- distinguish engagement from useful feedback
- follow-up action
- archive marketing asset
- what did we learn?

Marketing should measure **actions and learning**, not popularity.

## Interaction rule

Each page gets **one primary CTA at a time**.

Examples:
- Begin Creating
- Review My Studio
- Watch Demonstration
- Build My Website
- Prepare My Promotion

Secondary tools remain visually subordinate.

## Video rule

For teaching videos:
- learner-controlled playback
- chapters/segments
- captions/transcript
- no autoplay
- no background music unless instructionally required
- key cues immediately beside the relevant visual
- downloadable still/reference when useful

## Evidence rule

Every lesson ends with a concrete artifact.

Creative Day → artwork/process evidence  
CEO Day → decision/management record  
Skills Day → study/exercise/application  
Artpreneur Day → usable business asset  
Marketing Day → outreach/promotion/feedback record

## Mobile/tablet rule

The active task must remain usable on a tablet in portrait and landscape.

- minimum 16px body text
- no horizontal scrolling for core work
- touch targets sized comfortably
- one-column active learning flow on narrow screens
- media scales without hiding controls
- sticky navigation cannot cover evidence controls
- save/export actions remain visible but nonintrusive

## Accessibility

- captions/transcripts
- alt text guidance
- keyboard operability
- clear focus states
- sufficient contrast
- avoid color-only instructions
- plain-language directions
- downloadable alternatives where possible
- disclose required materials and technology before beginning

## Assessment philosophy

The five engines should not share one grading mechanism.

- Creative Day: evidence + reflection, not taste score
- CEO Day: completion/accuracy of management actions
- Skills Day: technique criteria + self-comparison + repeatability
- Artpreneur Day: usable asset meets functional checklist
- Marketing Day: outreach action + documentation + learning from response

80% mastery applies where knowledge/functional criteria are appropriate; artistic taste is not an 80% test.
