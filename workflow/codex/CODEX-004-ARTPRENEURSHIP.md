# Conversation Codex 004 · Artpreneurship Foundations

## Decision

The Arts program will teach **artpreneurship**: the practical business of building a sustainable working life around making and selling art.

Students will not study entrepreneurship as an abstract subject. They will build the actual infrastructure of an art career during Year 1.

## Required Year 1 artpreneurship assets

- simple artist website built through Khaemenes
- online portfolio
- artist bio and artist statements
- print-ready business card
- printer/vendor comparison
- artwork inventory
- pricing and cost worksheet
- opportunity tracker
- local exhibit / public art vending / market research
- artist-call application packet
- public feedback / market test
- simple sales/event kit
- Artpreneurship Growth Fund plan

## Website standard

Minimum pages:
- Home
- Portfolio
- About / Artist Statement
- Contact

Optional:
- Available Work / Shop
- Exhibitions
- Commissions
- News / Events

Students learn:
- simple page structure
- image preparation
- alt text
- mobile readability
- navigation
- contact methods
- domain/hosting awareness
- updating a portfolio
- privacy and scam awareness

## Business cards

Students learn how to:
- decide what belongs on the card
- keep it readable
- align it with artist identity
- prepare print-ready files
- understand resolution, bleed and safe area
- compare paper/finish/quantity
- compare at least three printers or ordering options
- calculate per-card cost

Buying cards is optional. The educational requirement is informed preparation and comparison.

## Finding opportunities

Students build an opportunity tracker for:
- galleries
- juried shows
- artist calls
- public art calls
- community shows
- street fairs
- makers markets
- art festivals
- library/community exhibits
- open studios
- pop-ups
- online juried opportunities

For each opportunity they record deadline, fees, eligibility, images required, statement/bio requirements, booth/commission costs, rules, and risk/reward notes.

## Selling practice

Students learn:
- price display
- inventory numbers
- payment options
- receipts/invoices
- packaging
- customer communication
- commission deposits
- fulfillment
- follow-up
- recordkeeping

Current tax, permit, insurance and vending rules must be verified for the actual location/event; the course does not give individualized legal or tax advice.

## Artpreneurship Growth Fund

Students learn to reinvest some art income into future creative and business capacity.

Possible categories:
- materials and tools
- prints/reproduction
- website/domain/hosting
- business cards/marketing
- booth/table/display
- frames/presentation
- shipping/packaging
- education/workshops
- emergency reserve

Students model low, medium and strong sales scenarios. Reinvestment percentages are chosen by the learner and are not prescribed financial advice.

## ARTS-151 revised 13-unit structure

1. Artist Career Models & Income Streams
2. Skills, Interests, Calling & Market Fit
3. Finding a Niche Without Trapping Yourself
4. Audience Discovery Through Real Conversations
5. Build Your Simple Artist Website
6. Build the Online Portfolio
7. Business Cards & Professional Identity
8. Pricing, Costs & Artpreneurship Growth Fund
9. Find Local Exhibits, Markets & Public Vending Events
10. Artist Calls, Applications & Submission Packets
11. Selling Art in Person & Online
12. Small Public Launch / Vending / Exhibit Experiment
13. Year-One Artpreneurship Plan

## Why this matters

The student should not finish Year 1 asking, “How do I start?”

They should already have a visible body of work, a public-facing presence, professional identity materials, an opportunity pipeline, basic sales systems and a plan for reinvesting in their own practice.

## Next step

Build these activities directly into the ARTS-151 lesson engine and connect the website/portfolio pieces to the portfolio course so students only create each asset once and improve it over time.
